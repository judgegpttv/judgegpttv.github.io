    Wildfire Intelligence Collaboration and Coordination Act of 2025

    S.453 would establish a Wildfire Intelligence Center involving the Departments of Agriculture, Commerce, and the Interior.

    Identifier
    S. 453
    Jurisdiction
    United States (Federal)
    Status
    Proposed federal legislation
    Dates
    S.453 was introduced in the U.S. Senate on February 6, 2025 and referred to the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry. It has not been enacted.
    Latest update
    February 6, 2025
    Update date
    2025-02-06

    What it does: S.453 would establish a Wildfire Intelligence Center involving the Departments of Agriculture, Commerce, and the Interior. The Center would combine wildfire science, technology, assessment, and prediction to support decisions involving pre-fire land and fuels management, community risk reduction, active wildfire response, and post-fire recovery. It would also promote information sharing among federal, state, Tribal, academic, and private-sector partners.

    Potential impact: S.453 would create a dedicated federal structure for bringing wildfire information and technology together to support decisions before, during, and after fires. For "Forestry Firewall", this raises a central emerging-technology question: Can better use of wildfire intelligence help government move from detecting risk to preventing and responding to fires more effectively?

    Fix Our Forests Act

    The Fix Our Forests Act proposes changes to forest management and wildfire-risk reduction, including expedited environmental review for certain projects and expanded use of wildfire assessment, prediction, mapping, and technology.

    Identifier
    H.R. 471 / S. 1462
    Jurisdiction
    United States (Federal)
    Status
    Proposed federal legislation
    Dates
    H.R.471 was introduced in the House on January 16, 2025 and passed the House on January 23, 2025. S.1462 was introduced in the Senate on April 10, 2025; on October 27, 2025, the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry reported an amended version and the bill was placed on the Senate Legislative Calendar. The legislation has not been enacted.
    Latest update
    October 27, 2025
    Update date
    2025-10-27

    What it does: The Fix Our Forests Act proposes changes to forest management and wildfire-risk reduction, including expedited environmental review for certain projects and expanded use of wildfire assessment, prediction, mapping, and technology. The legislation also proposes additional federal structures for wildfire intelligence and coordination.

    Potential impact: The legislation addresses two issues raised by "Forestry Firewall": how quickly preventive forest-management projects can proceed and how technology and wildfire information can support risk-reduction efforts. Its proposed changes also raise questions about how to balance faster preventive action with existing environmental-review, conservation, and public-interest protections.

    Sources

    • Congress.gov — S.1462, Fix Our Forests Act

    FLAME Act of 2009 / Cohesive Wildfire Management Strategy

    The FLAME Act requires the Departments of Agriculture and the Interior to jointly develop a cohesive wildfire-management strategy.

    Identifier
    43 U.S.C. § 1748b · Public Law 111-88
    Jurisdiction
    United States (Federal)
    Status
    Existing federal law
    Dates
    The FLAME Act of 2009 was enacted as Title V of Public Law 111-88 on October 30, 2009.
    Latest update
    October 30, 2009
    Update date
    2009-10-30

    What it does: The FLAME Act requires the Departments of Agriculture and the Interior to jointly develop a cohesive wildfire-management strategy. The strategy addresses matters including community risk, wildfire response, hazardous-fuel reduction, changing wildfire conditions, and prioritization of wildfire-management resources. Federal law also provides for periodic revision of the strategy as conditions change.

    Potential impact: The FLAME Act establishes an existing federal framework for coordinated and adaptive wildfire management, including prevention through community-risk assessment and hazardous-fuel reduction. For "Forestry Firewall", the question is therefore not whether a federal wildfire strategy exists, but whether existing policies and tools are keeping pace with changing wildfire risks and emerging technological capabilities.

    Sources

    • U.S. Code — 43 U.S.C. § 1748b
    • Congress.gov — Public Law 111-88

    Turning Wildfire Prevention Plans into Action

    Jurisdiction
    United States (Federal)
    Status
    Partial Gap

    What it does: Federal policy already provides for wildfire-risk assessment, hazardous-fuel reduction, prescribed fire, and other preventive forest-management activities. Proposed legislation would further streamline some regulatory processes. But identifying wildfire risk and authorizing preventive work do not necessarily ensure that preventive treatments occur at the needed pace and scale. Implementation can also depend on staffing, resources, operational capacity, and the ability to carry preventive measures through to completion before wildfire risk increases.

    Potential Policy Question

    Beyond regulatory streamlining, should federal policy do more to ensure that identified wildfire risks result in timely preventive action?

    Sources

    • U.S. Code — 43 U.S.C. § 1748b
  • Policy Gap Partial Gap

    Keeping Wildfire Technology Current

    Jurisdiction
    United States (Federal)
    Status
    Partial Gap

    What it does: Federal policy already provides for the use of technologies including unmanned aircraft, real-time fire mapping, resource tracking, wildfire decision-support systems, and tools for predicting where future wildfires may start. But wildfire technology continues to evolve. Emerging capabilities in artificial intelligence, autonomous systems, advanced sensors, satellite monitoring, predictive modeling, and other technologies may create new opportunities to prevent fires, monitor changing forest conditions, identify risks earlier, track active wildfires, and support firefighting operations. Federal wildfire-technology policy therefore faces an ongoing question of how existing frameworks should adapt as new capabilities emerge.

    Potential Policy Question

    How should federal policy keep pace with emerging technologies that could help prevent, detect, monitor, and combat wildfires?

    Sources

    • U.S. Code — 43 U.S.C. § 1748b-1, Wildfire Technology Modernization
